Tenet of the Spark is a cinematic action game where you seamlessly switch between 3 timelines — modern day, Viking age, and Aztec world — to get the edge in battle. Crush enemies, solve time-bending puzzles, and explore 3 distinct worlds full of rich history and unique characters. Developed by ROAR Games studio.

As a technical designer I was in charge of prototyping, developing, iterating, polishing, bug fixing, and optimization of player mechanics, AI archetypes, gameplay systems, and interactive content on levels.

Contents

    All videos show alpha footage from a work-in-progress build. Art, animation, UI, VFX, writing, audio, and engine code were created by the team. My part is the gameplay design and implementation described below.

    Combat Mechanics

    Design, prototyping, implementation, and iteration of the player's combat abilities. Each ability was built to reveal the personality and fighting style of its character: the Viking's brute strength, the Aztec's evasive ranged combat, and Will's agility and quick melee strikes.

    • Perfect block and perfect dodge abilities and windows
    • Charged attacks for each of the three worlds, with the Aztec charged attacks as my main focus
    • Various boomerang attacks: pull, double, round, chain
    • Pushing hammer strike
    • Ground slam attack

    For perfect block and dodge, I made a custom Anim Notify State that tells the Combat Manager when each timing window opens and closes, so the windows are set up directly on the animations.

    A compilation of combat ability prototypes for the Aztec and the Viking: pulling boomerang throw, pushing hammer strike, boomerang throw ricocheting between nearby enemies, double boomerang throw, and round boomerang throw.

    The Viking's special ground slam attack that deals area damage.

    AI Archetypes

    Development of enemy archetypes, each with its own behaviour, attack patterns, and abilities. For every archetype I set up the full package — animations, VFX, and SFX — and tuned it together with the animation, audio, and combat design teams until the fight felt right.

    • Buffer
    • Tank
    • Roller

    The Buffer archetype supports its allies and keeps its distance from the player. It's built with State Tree and EQS: level designers place points tagged as safe zones around the arena, and the Buffer moves to the one farthest from the player.

    It first heals allies whose health drops below a threshold, then buffs a random ally. Buffs are Gameplay Effects with their own Gameplay Cues. When there is no one left to support, the Buffer falls back to throwing its staff at the player as a projectile and becomes a regular melee fighter.

    Boss Fight

    Implementation of the game's boss encounter: a fight across three phases, with all three worlds available to the player throughout. I built each phase with its animations, VFX, and SFX in place, then iterated with the design, animation, and audio teams on the pacing, the transitions between phases, and the scripted beats that frame the fight.

    The first two phases of the boss fight, each with its own set of boss abilities. In the third phase, the boss fights as a regular melee enemy.

    Interactive Environment and Puzzles

    Implementation of the smart objects that level designers build encounters, traversal, and puzzles out of, along with the systems underneath them. A large part of this work was supporting level designers directly: scripting the setups they needed, and making the objects configurable enough for them to build and iterate on content.

    • Doors
    • Rotatable platform
    • Pushable cart
    • Flippable wagon
    • Overhead crane
    • Hanging platform
    • Puzzle toolkit and its components
    • Double wall jump
    • Pickups

    All interactive objects are Smart Objects, and the interaction with each of them is assembled in a State Tree from reusable tasks and conditions. Objects are connected through a separate puzzle actor: it listens to events from some objects (a generator turning on, a cart moving, a button being pressed) and drives the state of others (opening a door, lighting a lamp, and so on). Level designers build a puzzle by assigning object references in the puzzle actor's settings.

    Each object is a single actor with a different look in each timeline, while its state is shared across all three. Lighting a fire in the Aztec world turns on the generator in Will's world, and putting the fire out turns the generator off.

    • Fixed / static camera
    • Narrow space camera

    Cross-functional Collaboration and Technical Support

    For each feature I maintained close communication with the design, animation, audio, art, and engineering teams, taking it from conception to a shippable state and making sure gameplay elements were fun, polished, technically correct, and optimized. Alongside this I supported the technical game design needs of the project and acted as the bridge between the gameplay design and engineering teams — translating design intent into technical requirements, and engine constraints back into terms designers could work with.
