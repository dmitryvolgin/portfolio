Encased

Developer
Dark Crystal Games
Release Date
Sep 7, 2021
Steam Page
View on Steam →

Encased is an isometric role-playing game in a sci-fi post-apocalyptic setting developed by Dark Crystal Games studio. It was inspired by the first two Fallout games, Divinity: Original Sin, Baldur’s Gate, and the like.

The game is non-linear, allowing players to kill any in-game character, open any door and any chest, progress stealthily, play as a pacifist, play a low-intelligence character, work in a party, or go solo. Encased is highly systemic, where systems interact not only with the player but also with other systems and the game world around them.

As a gameplay scripter I was in charge of designing, documenting, prototyping, developing, iterating, bug fixing, and polishing systems, events, quests, puzzles, secrets, and combat encounters.

    Game Content Design and Scripting

    This involved designing and scripting quests, puzzles, combat encounters, random encounters, secrets, entities, and other interactive content within the game's locations. It was crucial to consider a multitude of gameplay options, all systems, and player builds when detailing events.

    Quest design for Encased is a unique challenge because the player has the freedom to kill any character, including main quest NPCs, and still progress through the game. Furthermore, the game unfolds in an open world, where the player can go anywhere and complete quests in any order.

    Crime System

    A complex system managing the world's response to the player's hostile or criminal actions.

    Every crime leaves behind evidence: physical objects in the world that NPCs literally discover. Once they find it, they start an investigation and patrol the surrounding area in search of the player. The severity of a crime is set by its most serious piece of evidence, with evidence severity defined in a config.

    If the player is spotted, NPCs respond based on that severity. The player's options include bribery, persuasion, intimidation, fines, combat, or imprisonment. In prison, all possessions are confiscated and a prisoner's uniform is provided; the player can either wait out the sentence or attempt an escape, which is also a crime.

    A case can also go cold: if no witness is left alive, it's closed, and if the player stays in disguise, hides, or leaves the location unseen, it's closed after a while. Otherwise, crimes lower the player's reputation with the faction, which can lead to ambushes, and settlement residents may avoid interacting with the player.

    During the development process, numerous quests and events were adapted to integrate seamlessly with the crime system.

    Reinforcement System

    If the player triggers a large-scale battle on an inhabited location, periodic reinforcements arrive to assist the inhabitants in combat. There are six primary factions in the game, each with its own set of reinforcements.

    Town Raids

    Gameplay implementation of the dangerous wastelands narrative. Also, it helps demonstrate how the game world functions on its own.

    Occasionally, settlements come under attack from raiders, wild animals, or hostile robots. The player can assist the inhabitants, earning gratitude from the locals and reputation with the faction. Alternatively, the player can use the opportunity to attack weakened settlers and take control of the settlement.

    Abandoned Towns

    World reactivity that lets players witness the consequences of their actions, built around the game's narrative of anomalies affecting the bodies of the dead.

    If more than 80% of a settlement's inhabitants die, the dead turn into hostile zombies that keep their names and all of their belongings. The transformation happens only when the player returns to the location, which gives them a chance to save the survivors first, including characters needed for quests. If any survivors remain, the player can help them fight the zombies or choose to leave. Quest characters who turn keep their quest items in the zombie's inventory, so the game always stays completable.

    Vehicles

    Vehicles allow the player to move faster on the global map, store items in the trunk, bypass certain random encounters, and honk the horn. While driving, the player encounters unique vehicle-related events. Vehicles are also integrated into the crime system: stealing or damaging a car belonging to another character is considered a crime.

    During the development process, numerous quests, events, and locations were adapted to integrate seamlessly with this system.
