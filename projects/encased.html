Encased is an isometric role-playing game in a sci-fi post-apocalyptic setting developed by Dark Crystal Games studio. It was inspired by the first two Fallout games, Divinity: Original Sin, Baldur’s Gate, and like.

The game is non-linear, allowing players to kill any in-game character, open any door and any chest, progress stealthily, play as a pacifist, act mentally powerless, work in a party, or go solo. Encased is highly systemic, where systems interact not only with the player but also with other systems and the game world around them.

As a gameplay scripter I was in charge of designing, documenting, prototyping, developing, iterating bug fixing, and polishing systems, events, quests, puzzles, secrets, and combat encounters.

Technologies I used on a daily basis:

  • Unity engine as a base
  • Custom in-house RPG framework
  • C# programming language.

    Crime System

    A complex system managing the world's response to hostile or criminal player’s actions.

    When the player commits a crime, it leaves behind evidence, prompting NPCs’ investigations. If the player is spotted, NPCs respond based on the severity of the crime. The player's choices include bribery, persuasion, intimidation, fines, combat, or imprisonment. In prison, all possessions are confiscated, and a prisoner's uniform is provided. The player can either wait out their sentence or attempt an escape, which is also considered a crime.

    Eventually, this leads to a decline in reputation with the faction, resulting in potential ambushes. In settlements, residents may avoid interaction with the player.

    During the development process, numerous quests and events were adapted to integrate seamlessly with the crime system.

    Reinforcement System

    If the player triggers a large-scale battle on an inhabited location, periodic reinforcements arrive to assist the inhabitants in combat. There are six primary factions in the game, each with its own set of reinforcements.

    Town Raids

    Gameplay implementation of the dangerous wastelands narrative. Also, it helps demonstrate how the game world functions on its own.

    Occasionally, settlements come under attack from raiders, wild animals, or hostile robots. The player can assist the inhabitants, earning gratitude from the locals and reputation with the faction. Alternatively, the player can use the opportunity to attack weakened settlers and take control of the settlement.

    Abandoned Towns

    World reactivity for players to witness the consequences of their actions. This system implements the narrative of anomalies affecting the bodies of the dead.

    If, for any reason, the majority of inhabitants in a settlement dies, the player returning to the location after some time will discover that the dead have transformed into hostile zombies. These zombies acquire names and inherit all the belongings of their predecessors. If there are still survivors in the settlement, the player can help them fight the zombies, or choose to leave.

    Vehicles

    Vehicles allow the player to move faster on the global map, store items in the trunk, bypass certain random encounters, and honk the horn. While driving, the player encounters unique vehicle-related events. Vehicles are also integrated into the crime system: stealing or damaging a car belonging to another character is considered a crime.

    During the development process, numerous quests, events, and locations were adapted to integrate seamlessly with this system.
